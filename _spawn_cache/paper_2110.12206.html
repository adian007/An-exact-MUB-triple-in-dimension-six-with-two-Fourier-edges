Abstract

The complete classification of 6×66\times 6 complex Hadamard matrices (CHMs) is a long-standing open problem. In this paper we investigate a series of CHMs, such as the CHMs containing a 2×32\times 3 submatrix with rank one, the CHMs containing exactly three distinct elements and all elements of the first row being one, the H2H_{2}-reducible matrices containing exactly three distinct matrix elements, and the CHMs containing a 3×33\times 3 Hadamard submatrix. We characterize all forms of these CHMs. Our results are the latest progress on the complete classification of CHMs.

  Introduction

For describing observables in quantum physics, Schwinger defined the MUBs schwinger60 in 1960. It is known that two quantum states in ℂd\mathbb{C}^{d} are mutually unbiased (MU) when their inner product has modulus 1d\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}. Two orthogonal bases in ℂd\mathbb{C}^{d} are MU when their elements are all MU. We refer to the complete MUBs as d+1d+1 orthogonal bases in ℂd\mathbb{C}^{d} and any two of them are MU. Ref.WOOTTERS1989363 found that the complete MUBs in dimension dd exist if dd is a prime power. So the first unknown case occurs at d=6d=6. People have studied this case by many methods, such as the average distance between four bases in dimension six rle11, Fourier family of Hadamard matrices jmm09, computer program bw09, noise robustness Designolle2018Quantifying, product basis mw12jpa135307; mw12jpa102001; mpw16; Chen2018Mutually and so on Boykin05; bw08; bw10; deb10; wpz11; mw12ijqi; Chen2017Product. This case is also a long-standing open problem.

We refer to the complex Hadamard matrix (CHM) H as an n×nn\times n matrix with elements of modulus one, and 1n\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}H is a unitary matrix. For convenience, we ignore the coefficient 1n\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}} of the CHM when we discuss four mutually unbiased bases (MUBs) containing the identity matrix in the following content. In this paper we investigate some 6×66\times 6 CHMs. We prove that all H2H_{2}-reducible matrices defined by Karlsson in karlsson11 containing exactly three distinct matrix elements are complex equivalent to the specific matrix M2M_{2} in (16). This is presented in Lemma 8. We find a new family of CHMs and we obtain that all 6×66\times 6 CHMs containing a 3×33\times 3 Hadamard submatrix are complex equivalent to the matrices from the new CHM family. They are the latest results on 6×66\times 6 CHMs and they might lead to the complete classification of 6×66\times 6 CHMs. TO BE CONTINUED.

  CHM

To find out the relation between different CHMs, we define the complex equivalence. We refer to the monomial unitary matrix as a unitary matrix each of whose rows and columns has exactly one nonzero entry. We say that two matrices UU and VV are complex equivalent when U=P​V​QU=PVQ where P,QP,Q are monomial unitary matrices. Specially when P,QP,Q are both permutation matrices, we say that UU and VV are equivalent. When P,QP,Q are both real monomial unitary matrices, we say that UU and VV are real equivalent. One can easily verify that if UU and VV are equivalent or real equivalent then UU and VV are complex equivalent.
