# Physics interpretation: why dimension six matters

MUBs are measurement settings whose outcome statistics are maximally complementary: for states from distinct bases, every transition probability is `1/6`. In quantum tomography, a complete set would provide a highly symmetric measurement design. In dimension six, the prime-power construction theorem does not apply because `6` is composite, making the existence and maximality questions structurally different rather than merely computationally difficult.

The complex-Hadamard formulation turns a measurement question into a constrained geometry problem on unitary matrices. The Fourier, Dita, Karlsson, and Tao examples represent different symmetry and deformation regimes. The local evidence that third-MUB loci are thin is physically meaningful: highly complementary measurement triples appear to require special interferometric phase relations, not generic parameters.

The fourth-MUB witness has a direct operational interpretation. A vector in `W1(H,B3)` would be one candidate outcome state compatible with a fourth maximally complementary measurement. Empty `W1` rules out even one such outcome, hence rules out the entire fourth basis. This is stronger than merely failing to assemble six vectors.

The correct physics conclusion is therefore cautious: the computations support maximality of several important measurement triples, while the universal dimension-six statement remains open.
