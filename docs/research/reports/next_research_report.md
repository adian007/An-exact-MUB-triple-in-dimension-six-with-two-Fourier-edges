# Next research report

## Research question

Can the isolated Dita-point fourth-MUB obstructions be upgraded to a theorem on a nontrivial parameter interval, and eventually to the certified covering region R?

## Highest-value experiment

Choose a compact Dita subarc that avoids the four constellation-change windows. For each endpoint and midpoint:

1. regenerate the MU pool;
2. match roots projectively across lambda;
3. enumerate all third cliques;
4. quantify the W1 exclusion margin;
5. attempt interval subdivision in lambda.

A positive margin that survives subdivision would be the first concrete bridge from point certificates to a region theorem.

## Algebraic parallel

At one generic Dita lambda, export the exact coefficient field and compute a saturated elimination ideal for W1. Compare its degree and factor structure with the D0-equivalent special point. This determines whether the special exact certificate is representative or structurally exceptional.

## Stop conditions

Stop and label the route inconclusive if:

- pool completeness fails near a box boundary;
- the B3 branch cannot be transported with a certified error bound;
- interval widths erase the W1 margin;
- elimination is performed over inexact floating coefficients.

## Expected outcome

The most realistic near-term result is a rigorous theorem on selected subarcs or fixed CHM-equivalence classes, not a global proof. That would still materially improve the project because it would identify which obstruction mechanism persists under parameter variation.
