\section{Numerical-interval endpoint tests on the Dita slice (T3)}
\label{sec:proof-t3}

\begin{lemma}[CHM equivalence transfers $W_1$-emptiness]\label{lem:chm-w1}
Suppose $H_1,H_2\in K_6^{(3)}$ are CHM-equivalent via diagonal unitaries $D_r,D_c$ and a column permutation $P$, in the sense that after the standard dephasing gauge
\[
\mathrm{dephase}(H_1)=D_r\,\mathrm{dephase}(H_2)\,P\,D_c.
\]
Let $B_3$ be an orthonormal $6\times6$ matrix whose columns are mutually unbiased to $\mathbf{I}$ and to the columns of $H_2$, and set $B_3':=D_r B_3$ (columnwise, then renormalized).
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item the columns of $B_3'$ are orthonormal and mutually unbiased to $\mathbf{I}$ and to the columns of $H_1$ (hence $B_3'$ is a third MUB for $\{\mathbf{I},H_1\}$);
    \item a unit vector $v$ solves $W_1(H_2,B_3)$ if and only if $D_r v$ solves $W_1(H_1,B_3')$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, $W_1(H_2,B_3)$ is empty if and only if $W_1(H_1,B_3')$ is empty.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Diagonal unitaries act entrywise by phases, so $| (D_r v)_i |=|v_i|$ and $\langle D_r u, D_r w\rangle=\langle u,w\rangle$.
Mutual unbiasedness to $\mathbf{I}$ and pairwise orthogonality are therefore preserved by $v\mapsto D_r v$ and $B_3\mapsto B_3'$.
For mutual unbiasedness to the Hadamard: the CHM relation identifies columns of (dephased) $H_1$ with phased, permuted columns of (dephased) $H_2$ left-multiplied by $D_r$, so
\[
\bigl|\langle D_r v,\, (H_1)_{:,k}\rangle\bigr|
=
\bigl|\langle v,\, (H_2)_{:,p(k)}\rangle\bigr|
\]
up to the dephasing gauge (itself diagonal phases).
Thus MU-to-$H$ and MU-to-$B_3$ pass to MU-to-$H_1$ and MU-to-$B_3'$, and conversely.
\end{proof}

\begin{finding}[T3: square-system endpoint tests at seven Dita-$\lambda$ points]
\label{thm:T3}
\label{claim:T3}
Let $\theta_D=\arccos(1/\sqrt{3})$, $\phi=\pi/4$, and
\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{cert}}:=\Bigl\{0,\ 0.4,\ \tfrac{\pi}{3},\ \tfrac{2\pi}{3},\ \pi,\ \tfrac{4\pi}{3},\ \tfrac{5\pi}{3}\Bigr\}.
\]
Write $H(\lambda)=H(\theta_D,\phi,\lambda)$.
For every $\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{cert}}$ and every size-$6$ clique $B_3$ in the numerically recovered MU pool of $\{\mathbf{I},H(\lambda)\}$ (\S\ref{sec:methods}), the saved HomotopyContinuation runs tracked and certified all $252$ paths of the selected square system and found no endpoint passing the original $W_1$ residual test. This is a numerical-interval endpoint-test finding for $40/40$ recovered-pool cases; it does not prove $W_1$ empty or that these triples lack a fourth MUB.
\end{finding}

\paragraph{Logical relation.}
A fourth MUB $B_4=\{v_1,\ldots,v_6\}$ extending $\{\mathbf{I},H,B_3\}$ consists of six orthonormal vectors, each mutually unbiased to every column of $\mathbf{I}$, $H$, and $B_3$.
After a global phase fixing the first coordinate positive, each $v_j$ is a solution of the single-vector witness $W_1(H,B_3)$:
five unimodular gauge equations $|z_i|=1$ ($i=1,\ldots,5$) together with mutual-unbiasedness to the six columns of $H$ and of $B_3$ ($17$ equations in $10$ complex variables; \repo{build\_numeric\_fourth\_mub\_witness\_system}, $n_{\mathrm{wit}}=1$).
Hence a fourth MUB implies feasibility of $W_1$. The endpoint tests below do not establish that $W_1$ is empty.

\paragraph{Procedure and recorded results.}
Form a generic square subsystem of $W_1$ by ten random complex linear combinations of the seventeen equations (Bertini / HomotopyContinuation pattern; fixed seed), compute its mixed volume ($252$ in every run below), track all $252$ start paths, call HomotopyContinuation's interval-arithmetic \texttt{certify()} on every nonsingular endpoint, and residual-check each certified square-root against the full overdetermined system (tolerance $10^{-8}$).
For each submitted $(H,B_3)$ the saved accounting is
\[
N_{\mathrm{MV(square)}}=N_{\mathrm{tracked}}=N_{\mathrm{certified}}=252,\qquad
N_{\mathrm{full\text{-}residual\ pass}}=0.
\]

The $252$ start-path count is computed for the actual square system after the random linear combinations; the code separately computes the mixed volume of the original $17$-equation system, which is not used as that path count. Tracking and certifying all reported endpoints establishes local certificates for those isolated square-system roots. It does not establish that the square system has no singular or positive-dimensional components, nor that a positive-dimensional component of $W_1$ is absent. The residual filter is evaluated numerically at the tracked endpoints. Accordingly, $N_{\mathrm{full\text{-}residual\ pass}}=0$ means no tracked endpoint passed that tolerance test; it is not a proof that $W_1=\varnothing$.

\textbf{Native all-recovered-pool-clique endpoint tests at seven $\lambda$.}
At each $\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{cert}}$, every distinct size-$6$ clique in the recovered MU pool was submitted to the method above:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\lambda & \#\{6\text{-cliques}\} & \text{no residual pass}\\
\hline
0 & 10 & 10/10\\
0.4 & 4 & 4/4\\
\pi/3 & 4 & 4/4\\
2\pi/3 & 4 & 4/4\\
\pi & 10 & 10/10\\
4\pi/3 & 4 & 4/4\\
5\pi/3 & 4 & 4/4
\end{array}
\]
Total: $\mathbf{40/40}$ had no full-system residual pass among the tracked endpoints (\repo{results/certify\_nwit1\_all\_cliques\_four\_classes.txt}, \repo{results/certify\_nwit1\_all\_cliques\_lambdapi.txt}). The combinatorial pool test also reported \texttt{found\_fourth=false}; neither observation is an exact or global emptiness proof.

\textbf{CHM-class structure (independence, not inheritance).}
The $720$-permutation / diagonal-phase search (\repo{chm\_equivalence\_residual}) numerically matches $H(\lambda)$ and $H(\lambda+\pi)$ on this slice (residuals $\sim10^{-15}$), while
$\{H(0),H(0.4),H(\pi/3),H(2\pi/3)\}$ are pairwise CHM-inequivalent (minimum residual $\gtrsim0.49$; \repo{results/verify\_nwit1\_referee\_checks.txt}), as recorded in Lemma~\ref{lem:L4}.
The seven points therefore have four numerically distinguished CHM classes; the native $40/40$ endpoint-test record is independent at each $\lambda$ (not deduced from representatives).
As corroboration, CHM transport of every clique at $\{0,\pi/3,2\pi/3\}$ to $\{\pi,4\pi/3,5\pi/3\}$ also yields $18/18$ empty (\repo{results/verify\_chm\_b3\_transfer.txt}; Lemma~\ref{lem:chm-w1}).

\textbf{Clique-index nondeterminism.}
Among equal-length $6$-cliques, Graphs.jl enumeration order need not repeat across re-solves, so a single \texttt{argmax(|C|)} index set is not a stable label.
The $40/40$ endpoint-test record removes dependence on that choice within each recovered pool by ranging over every size-$6$ clique there.

\begin{remark}[Scope --- what is and is not claimed]
Finding~\ref{thm:T3} records square-system endpoint tests at seven explicit Dita-$\lambda$ values (four numerically distinguished CHM classes), covering every size-$6$ clique in each recovered pool ($40/40$).
``Every size-$6$ clique'' means every such clique in the MU pool recovered by the per-$H$ homotopy at that run (pool sizes $120$ at $\lambda{=}0,\pi$ and $72$ at the other five listed $\lambda$); it does not by itself certify against hypothetical third MUBs missed by an incomplete root track.
The $72$/$120$ split is a $\lambda$-dependent pool-size invariant of the pair $\{\mathbf{I},H(\lambda)\}$, not a pipeline artifact: the 2026-09 regression benchmarks (companion report) show the pool carries $120$ vectors in four $\lambda$-intervals of width ${\approx}0.22$ rad centered at $\lambda\in\{0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2\}$ (mod $2\pi$) and $72$ vectors elsewhere on the circle, with $10$ third bases in the $120$-regions and $4$ in the $72$-regions, and with deduplicated counts stable under a $10^{-6}$--$10^{-12}$ dedup-tolerance sweep; the benchmark third-basis counts ($10$ at $\lambda{=}\pi/2$, $4$ at $\lambda{=}0.4$) match the clique counts in the proof above.
At $\lambda{=}\pi/2$, the benchmark numerically identifies a CHM-equivalent representative of $D_0$ and reports no endpoint residual pass for all $10/10$ recovered cliques; this is not a proof that $W_1$ is empty. The $40/40$ tests likewise do not prove fourth-MUB absence at their inputs.
These findings are \textbf{not} a symbolic identity for all $\lambda\in[0,2\pi)$, not a theorem on the covering region $\mathcal{R}$ defined in the companion methods report (Paper~2), and not a statement about $N(6)$ or CHMs outside $K_6^{(3)}$.
A separate dense probe reports no fourth MUB at $628/628$ samples on $[0,2\pi]$; that numerical evidence is documented in Paper~2 and is not a proof of non-existence.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Pointer to the central open obstacle]\label{rem:m2-inexact}
The two-vector Macaulay2 run over \texttt{CC} ($\dim I=-1$ with an inexact-field warning) is \textbf{not} a certificate and is \textbf{not} confirmatory of T3.
T3 uses $n_{\mathrm{wit}}=1$ (mixed volume $252$) instead.
The failed exact-elimination attempt is the central obstacle to a theorem on all of $\mathcal{R}$; see \S\ref{sec:symbolic} and the companion methods audit (Paper~2).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Exact-coefficient $W_1$ emptiness at the $D_0$-equivalent pair (T4)]
\label{thm:T4}
Let $D_{\mathrm{bc}}$ be the block-circulant representative of the Di\c{t}a matrix $D_0$ and $F_D$ the closed-form third MUB for $\{\mathbf{I},D_{\mathrm{bc}}\}$ (Bengtsson--Bruzda--Ericsson--Larsson--Tadej--\.Zyczkowski 2007, eqs.~(79)--(80) and (78),(61)), both with entries in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{24},\sqrt{5})$.
Then the single-vector witness ideal of $W_1(D_{\mathrm{bc}},F_D)$ --- seventeen generators (five unimodular relations $z_iw_i=1$ and twelve mutual-unbiasedness products) in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{24},\sqrt5)[z_1,\ldots,z_5,w_1,\ldots,w_5]$; \repo{symbolic\_export/w1\_D0\_exact.m2} --- is the \emph{unit ideal}.
Consequently no flat vector is mutually unbiased to $\mathbf{I}$, $D_{\mathrm{bc}}$, and $F_D$ simultaneously: the MUB triple $\{\mathbf{I},D_{\mathrm{bc}},F_D\}$ does not extend by even a single vector of a fourth MUB.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Logical status.}
If a fourth MUB extended this triple, each of its vectors would satisfy $W_1(D_{\mathrm{bc}},F_D)$. Here $W_1$ is proved empty by exact unit-ideal membership over a number field --- a Gr\"obner certificate, not a homotopy \texttt{certify()} run.

\textbf{Field validity.}
$[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{24},\sqrt5):\mathbb{Q}]=16$: $\Phi_{24}(x)=x^8-x^4+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, and $5$ is not a square in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{24})$ --- certified by the split prime $p=73$ ($p\equiv1\bmod 24$, so $\Phi_{24}$ splits completely mod $73$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{24})$ embeds in residue fields $\mathbb{F}_{73}$, where $5$ is a quadratic non-residue).
Hence Macaulay2's \texttt{toField} quotient $\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{zt},s_5]/(\Phi_{24}(\mathrm{zt}),\,s_5^2-5)$ is genuinely a field and Gr\"obner arithmetic over it involves no zero divisors (\repo{scripts/julia/verify\_field\_degree\_w1.jl}; \repo{results/track\_c\_elimination/w1\_D0\_field\_degree.txt}).

\textbf{Method and verification.}
Macaulay2~1.24.05, bounded cost probe first, then the certificate run:
\[
\begin{array}{l|l|l|r}
\text{run} & \text{field} & \text{result} & \text{wall}\\
\hline
\text{probe (}\zeta_{24}\mapsto2,\ \sqrt5\mapsto103\text{)} & \mathbb{Z}/241 & \text{unit ideal (signal only)} & 0.12\,\mathrm{s}\\
\text{bounded \texttt{dim}} & \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{24},\sqrt5) & \dim = -1 & 29.0\,\mathrm{s}\\
\text{certificate} & \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{24},\sqrt5) & \mathrm{GB}=\{1\},\ 1\,\%\,I = 0 & 16.0\,\mathrm{s}
\end{array}
\]
The certificate run computes the full Gr\"obner basis (\texttt{gens gb}), which is the single element $1$, and confirms $1\,\%\,I=0$ (\repo{symbolic\_export/w1\_D0\_groebner.m2}; \repo{results/track\_c\_elimination/w1\_D0\_groebner.log}, \repo{w1\_D0\_cost\_estimate.log}).
No inexact-field warning arises (contrast Remark~\ref{rem:m2-inexact}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:T4} --- what is and is not claimed]
\label{rem:T4-scope}
(i) \emph{Point scope.} The exact statement is at the pair $(D_{\mathrm{bc}},F_D)$ itself. The repository's finite CHM search found numerical matches between $H(\theta_D,\pi/4,\lambda)$ and $D_0$/$D_{\mathrm{bc}}$ at $\lambda\in\{\pi/2,\,3\pi/2\}$ (dephased residuals $\sim10^{-13}$; \repo{results/identify\_d0\_in\_karlsson.txt}). It found no match at the other sampled T3 values, but this finite numerical search does not prove that these are the only Karlsson parameters equivalent to $D_0$. In particular, no canonical-Di\c{t}a parameter symmetry is used to infer an equivalence in this Karlsson parameterization. The exact T4 statement remains about $(D_{\mathrm{bc}},F_D)$; placing it on the Karlsson slice is numerical.

(ii) \emph{Clique scope.} Coverage is the single third basis $F_D$; this is \textbf{not} the $40/40$ recovered-pool endpoint-test sweep of Finding~\ref{thm:T3}, and it is not a theorem on the Di\c{t}a circle, on $\mathcal{R}$, or on all of $K_6^{(3)}$.

(iii) \emph{Object / method / result vs Brierley--Weigert (2009).} Headline: Theorem~\ref{thm:T4} \emph{reproves} a fact BW already established, by an independent exact method; it does \textbf{not} extend BW. Asymmetry: T4 is \emph{weaker in coverage} (one third basis $F_D$ vs BW's full pool $N_v{=}120$, $N_t{=}10$) and \emph{stronger in one narrow sense} (exact symbolic unit-ideal certificate for the fixed-$(H,B_3)$ witness $W_1$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{24},\sqrt5)$, versus BW's Groebner-on-pool construction plus combinatorial $N_p{=}0$). $D_{\mathrm{bc}}$ is CHM-equivalent to textbook $D_0$, and $F_D$ is Bengtsson's closed-form third MUB---neither object is new. BW's $N_p{=}0$ already implies $W_1(D_0,B_3)=\emptyset$ for every third basis from the pool, so the mathematical result at this point is corollary-grade; the value here is a machine-checkable cross-check on that instance via a different ideal---a pipeline-validation result, not a co-equal headline with Theorem~\ref{thm:T1}. A direct $W_1$ unit-ideal certificate at $\lambda=0$ remains open. At $\lambda=\pi/3$, Theorem~\ref{thm:T5} gives an exact Fourier-family identification for one specified third basis, from which the published theorem yields the fixed-triple non-extension corollary; it does not certify $W_1$ emptiness for all possible third bases.

(iv) \emph{Numerical counterpart (2026-09).} At the Karlsson input $\lambda=\pi/2$, the audited pipeline numerically reproduces BW's pool counts ($N_v{=}120$, $N_t{=}10$, $N_p{=}0$) and reports no full-system residual pass for $10/10$ witness endpoint tests. The CHM match to $D_0$ is numerical (Remark~\ref{rem:T4-scope}(i)); the endpoint tests are not proofs of $W_1$ emptiness. These data do not transfer T4's exact $\{1\}$ certificate from $(D_{\mathrm{bc}},F_D)$ to the Karlsson input.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact Fourier-family reduction for one $\pi/3$ triple (T5)}
\label{sec:proof-t5}

\begin{theorem}[Exact non-extendability of a specified algebraic triple]
\label{thm:T5}
Let $q=e^{i\pi/18}$, so $q^{12}-q^6+1=0$. Choose $a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}$ satisfying
\begin{align*}
323a^2&+16q^{11}-35q^{10}+170q^9-18q^8+120q^7+32q^6\\
&-166q^5-157q^4+8q^3-46q^2-48q-66=0,\\
323b^2&-166q^{11}+192q^{10}+170q^9-46q^8-72q^7+32q^6\\
&+150q^5-35q^4+8q^3+64q^2+120q-66=0,\\
323c^2&+150q^{11}-157q^{10}+170q^9+64q^8-48q^7+32q^6\\
&+16q^5+192q^4+8q^3-18q^2-72q-66=0.
\end{align*}
Let $H$ be the unnormalised Di\c{t}a matrix
\[
H=\begin{bmatrix}
1&1&1&1&1&1\\
1&-1&q^6&-q^6&q^9&-q^9\\
1&-q^9&q^9&q^9&-q^9&-1\\
1&q^9&-q^6&q^6&-1&-q^9\\
1&q^{-6}&-q^9&-1&-q^{-6}&q^9\\
1&-q^{-6}&-1&-q^9&q^{-6}&q^9
\end{bmatrix},
\]
and let $B_3=M/\sqrt6$, where $M=[v_0\ \cdots\ v_5]$ and
\begin{align*}
v_0&=(1,a,-qa,q^{-10},q^{-16}a,q^{-8})^T,&
v_1&=(1,-a,qa,q^{-10},q^2a,q^{-8})^T,\\
v_2&=(1,b,q^7b,q^2,q^{-4}b,q^{16})^T,&
v_3&=(1,-b,-q^7b,q^2,-q^{-4}b,q^{16})^T,\\
v_4&=(1,c,q^{-5}c,q^{14},q^8c,q^4)^T,&
v_5&=(1,-c,-q^{-5}c,q^{14},-q^8c,q^4)^T.
\end{align*}
Then $\{\mathbf{I},H/\sqrt6,B_3\}$ is a mutually unbiased triple and cannot be extended to a fourth mutually unbiased basis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{The exact triple.} The three quadratic equations are the phase-specific degree-two gcds of the common phase polynomial and the corresponding linear-in-$q$ relations in the exact coefficient-field certificate (\repo{docs/research/reports/phase2\_w1\_coefficient\_field\_2026-10-01.md}). Exact polynomial reduction gives $v_i^\dagger v_j=0$ for all $i\ne j$, $|v_{i,k}|=1$ for every entry, and $|h_j^\dagger v_i|^2=6$ for every pair of columns $h_j$ of $H$ and $v_i$ of $M$. Thus $H/\sqrt6$ and $B_3$ are orthonormal bases, both unbiased to the computational basis, and unbiased to one another. The checks are reproducible with \repo{scripts/python/derive\_pi3\_pairwise\_relations.py}, \repo{scripts/python/verify\_pi3\_exact\_remainders.py}, and \repo{scripts/python/prove\_pi3\_fourier\_exact\_direct.py}: they return zero remainders for all $15$ pairwise inner products, all $36$ unbiasedness equations, and all $36$ Fourier-chart entries, respectively. The phase roots need no sign convention: changing the sign of any one of $a,b,c$ swaps the corresponding two columns of $M$.

\textbf{Exact Fourier chart.} Use the zero-based row and column orders
\[
p=(4,3,1,0,2,5),\qquad s=(2,0,4,3,1,5),
\]
and let $N_{ij}=M_{p_i,s_j}$. Its dephased chart
\[
C_{ij}=\frac{N_{ij}N_{00}}{N_{i0}N_{0j}}
\]
is exactly
\[
C=\begin{bmatrix}
1&1&1&1&1&1\\
1&r&t&-1&-r&-t\\
1&\omega&\omega^2&1&\omega&\omega^2\\
1&\omega r&\omega^2t&-1&-\omega r&-\omega^2t\\
1&\omega^2&\omega&1&\omega^2&\omega\\
1&\omega^2r&\omega t&-1&\zeta r&\zeta^{-1}t
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad r=\frac ba,\quad t=\frac bc,\quad \zeta=q^6,\quad \omega=q^{12}.
\]
Here $\zeta^2=\omega$, $\zeta^3=-1$, and $\omega^3=1$. The displayed matrix is the unnormalised repository Fourier family with parameters
\[
x=\frac{b}{q^6a}=q^{30}r,\qquad y=\frac{b}{q^{12}c}=q^{24}t.
\]
The phase-modulus identities $|a|=|b|=|c|=1$ follow exactly from $d_i(q)d_i(q^{-1})=1$, where $a^2=d_a(q)$, $b^2=d_b(q)$, and $c^2=d_c(q)$ are the displayed quadratic relations. Each $d_i$ has rational coefficients and $|q|=1$, so $d_i(q^{-1})=\overline{d_i(q)}$; thus $|a|^2=|d_a(q)|=1$, and likewise for $b,c$. Hence $|x|=|y|=1$. Dephasing and the two permutations are row and column monomial operations, so the transition matrix of $(\mathbf{I},B_3)$ is equivalent to this Fourier-family member.

For an entry-by-entry comparison with Eq.~(3) of Jaming et al., put $\rho=q^6=e^{i\pi/3}$ and $\omega=\rho^2=e^{2\pi i/3}$. With $x=r/\rho=b/(q^6a)$ and $y=t/\omega=b/(q^{12}c)$, transposing the displayed $C$ gives
\[
C^T=\begin{bmatrix}
1&1&1&1&1&1\\
1&\rho x&\rho^2&\rho^3x&\rho^4&\rho^5x\\
1&\rho^2y&\rho^4&y&\rho^2&\rho^4y\\
1&\rho^3&1&\rho^3&1&\rho^3\\
1&\rho^4x&\rho^2&x&\rho^4&\rho^2x\\
1&\rho^5y&\rho^4&\rho^3y&\rho^2&\rho y
\end{bmatrix}=\sqrt6\,F(\alpha,\beta),
\qquad x=e^{2\pi i\alpha},\quad y=e^{2\pi i\beta}.
\]
This is the paper's Eq.~(3), entry by entry; its parameters range over $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$.

\textbf{Apply the pair obstruction.} Theorem~1.4 of Jaming et al.~\cite{Jaming2009} states verbatim: \begin{quote}\emph{None of the pairs $(I_d,F(a,b))$ of mutually unbiased orthonormal bases can be extended to a quartet $(I_d,F(a,b),C,D)$ of mutually unbiased orthonormal bases.}\end{quote} Here $a,b$ range over all real parameters modulo $1$, equivalently over every $|x|=|y|=1$ in Eq.~(3).

The row and column permutations and dephasing phases taking $M/\sqrt6$ to $C/\sqrt6$ preserve pair extendability: a left monomial unitary transports both bases and sends the computational basis to itself as an unordered basis; right monomial factors only reorder and rephase the columns of the other basis. It remains to justify the transpose orientation. For any unitary $U$, if $(\mathbf{I},U,X,Y)$ is a quartet, applying the same ambient unitary $U^\dagger$ to every basis and reordering gives $(\mathbf{I},U^\dagger,U^\dagger X,U^\dagger Y)$. Complex-conjugating every basis then gives a quartet containing $(\mathbf{I},\overline{U^\dagger})=(\mathbf{I},U^T)$. Both operations are reversible, so $(\mathbf{I},U)$ extends if and only if $(\mathbf{I},U^T)$ extends. Taking $U=C/\sqrt6$, the entrywise identity above identifies $U^T$ with Jaming et al.'s $F(\alpha,\beta)$. Their theorem therefore excludes a quartet containing $(\mathbf{I},B_3)$, and hence excludes a fourth basis extending the stated triple.

The conclusion is specific to this algebraically defined triple. The 72-vector pool and its recovered cliques are not used to prove the theorem, and no global non-existence statement for four MUBs in $\mathbb{C}^6$ follows. The high-precision comparison that motivated the algebraic ansatz is not an exact embedding-to-clique match, so T5 does not assert that this triple is one of the recovered cliques.
\end{proof}
