\begin{abstract}
We give an explicit algebraic triple of mutually unbiased bases (MUBs) in $\C^6$ containing the computational basis and a member of the \Dita{} slice of Karlsson's three-parameter family. Exact calculations identify the other two transition matrices as transposed members of the two-parameter Fourier family. Jaming et al.'s theorem therefore shows that both corresponding pairs are non-extendable, and in particular that this specific triple cannot be extended to four MUBs. We also give a closed form $D(z)$ for the whole \Dita{} slice and exactly count its Hadamard $2\times2$ submatrices: 51 generically and 75 at $z=1$. We do not infer non-extendability of the \Dita{} edge from this count: the published count criterion used for that inference has been challenged, and its authors' reply says that the disputed theorem is not presently established. Numerical continuation at seven slice parameters recovers 40 third bases; all show the same Fourier--Fourier pattern to residual at most $5\times10^{-15}$. This is numerical evidence only and motivates a structural conjecture. We also record a transcription discrepancy in a recent review and the degeneracy of Karlsson's M\"obius parametrisation on the slices used here.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} mutually unbiased bases; complex Hadamard matrices; Fourier family; \Dita{} family; Karlsson family; $H_2$-reducible matrices; exact algebraic computation.

\noindent\textbf{MSC 2020:} 05B20, 15B34, 81P15.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%=====================================================================

Orthonormal bases $\mathcal B_0,\dots,\mathcal B_{m-1}$ of $\C^d$ are \emph{mutually unbiased} if $|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|^2=1/d$ whenever $\psi$ and $\phi$ belong to different bases. Complete sets of $d+1$ MUBs exist in every prime-power dimension~\cite{Ivanovic1981,Wootters1989}, but in dimension six it is still unknown whether even four exist. Zauner's conjecture asserts that the maximum is three~\cite{Zauner1999}; see the review of McNulty and Weigert~\cite{McNultyWeigert2026} for the current state of the problem.

Fixing one basis to be the computational basis $\I$, every further basis unbiased to $\I$ is the column set of $H/\sqrt6$ for a complex Hadamard matrix (CHM) $H$, i.e. a $6\times6$ matrix with unimodular entries and $HH\dg=6\I$. The theorem of Jaming et al. excludes every pair $(\I,F(a,b))$ from a MUB quartet when $F(a,b)$ belongs to the two-parameter Fourier family~\cite{Jaming2009}.

Several benchmark cases are already understood. Grassl classified the vectors unbiased to $\{\I,F_6\}$; they give sixteen third bases, each maximal as a MUB triple~\cite{Grassl2004}. For the $D_6$ example, Brierley and Weigert report 120 vectors and ten third bases, building on the complete vector set identified by Bengtsson et al.~\cite{Brierley2009,BengtssonMUH2007}. We use these cases only as checks on the numerical pipeline, not as new results.

A submatrix-count obstruction was stated by Chen, Liang, Hu and Chen~\cite{Chen2021}. McNulty and Weigert argue that a lemma needed for the later count exclusions is incorrectly proved~\cite{McNultyWeigert2025Comment}. Liang and Chen's reply establishes some related count restrictions but says they cannot presently establish the disputed exclusion theorem~\cite{LiangChen2025Reply}. We therefore do not infer non-extendability from the number of Hadamard $2\times2$ submatrices; see Remark~\ref{rem:chen-status}.

Karlsson's three-parameter family $K_6^{(3)}$~\cite{Karlsson2011} consists of all $H_2$-reducible CHMs of order six~\cite{KarlssonH2}. It contains the Fourier and \Dita{} families as subfamilies, and the review~\cite{McNultyWeigert2026} records that $D_6^{(1)}$ is recovered on the slice $(\theta,\phi)=(\arccos(1/\sqrt3),\pi/4)$. Numerical searches for MUB triples inside $K_6^{(3)}$ were carried out by Brierley and Weigert~\cite{Brierley2009}, Goyeneche~\cite{Goyeneche2013} and Maxwell and Brierley~\cite{MaxwellBrierley2015}. More recently, Matolcsi, Matszangosz, Varga and Weiner encoded MUB triples by ``Hadamard cubes''~\cite{Matolcsi2025}, and Matszangosz and Sz\"oll\H{o}si gave an exact recognition criterion for the transposed Fourier family~\cite{MatszangoszSzollosi2024}. Both works make it natural to ask \emph{which families of CHMs can occur together as the three edges of a MUB triple}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An exact triple with two Fourier edges} (\cref{thm:main}). We specify an algebraic triple $\{\I,D(\rho)/\sqrt6,B\}$, with $\rho=e^{i\pi/3}$ and $D(\rho)$ on the \Dita{} slice. Both transitions involving $B$ are, after exact monomial operations and transposition, members of the Fourier family $F(a,b)$~\cite{Jaming2009}.
\item \textbf{Two exact non-extendability conclusions} (\cref{cor:three-edges}). Each of those two Fourier-family pairs is non-extendable by Jaming et al.; consequently this particular triple cannot be extended to a quartet. We make no non-extendability claim here for the third pair $(\I,D(\rho)/\sqrt6)$.
\item \textbf{An exact description of the \Dita{} slice} (\cref{prop:slice}). We give a closed form $D(z)$ for $|z|=1$, independent of Karlsson's degenerate M\"obius relations, and count its Hadamard $2\times2$ submatrices exactly. The count is not used as an extension obstruction.
\item \textbf{Numerical evidence and a conjecture} (\cref{sec:numerics}). At seven slice parameters, 40 recovered third bases have Fourier-family transitions to both $\I$ and $D(z)$ to fitting residual at most $5\times10^{-15}$. This motivates \cref{conj:triangle}; it does not prove exact membership or pool completeness.
\item \textbf{A literature-status correction} (\cref{rem:chen-status,app:erratum}). We explain why the disputed submatrix-count theorem is not invoked and record a transcription discrepancy in the displayed $A$-block of a recent review.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What is and is not new.} The exact triple and the two Fourier-family edge identifications are the main contribution. The closed form and submatrix count for $D(z)$ are exact structural information, but the count alone does not establish non-extendability of $(\I,D(z)/\sqrt6)$ in light of the status of the cited criterion. The numerical data do not settle that question for the whole slice or for generic members of $K_6^{(3)}$. At the sample point $K_6(0.3,0.5,0.2)$ we count nine Hadamard $2\times2$ submatrices; this single sample does not establish a general count or an extension obstruction.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}
%=====================================================================

\subsection{Equivalence and extendability of pairs}

Two CHMs are \emph{equivalent}, $H\simeq H'$, if $H'=P_1\Delta_1H\Delta_2P_2$ with permutation matrices $P_i$ and unitary diagonal matrices $\Delta_i$. A pair of orthonormal bases (written as unitary matrices) $(U_1,U_2)$ is \emph{extendable} if there are unitaries $U_3,U_4$ such that $\{U_1,\dots,U_4\}$ is a MUB quartet.

\begin{lemma}[Operations preserving extendability]\label{lem:pair-ops}
Let $U$ be a unitary matrix that is unbiased to $\I$. Each of the following replacements leaves the extendability of the pair $(\I,U)$ unchanged:
(i) $U\mapsto P_1\Delta_1U\Delta_2P_2$;
(ii) $U\mapsto U\dg$;
(iii) $U\mapsto\overline U$;
(iv) $U\mapsto U^{T}$.
Moreover, for unitaries $V,U$ unbiased to each other, $(V,U)$ is extendable if and only if $(\I,V\dg U)$ is.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Applying one fixed unitary to every basis preserves all overlaps, and so does reordering or rephasing the vectors inside a basis. For (i), left multiplication by $P_1\Delta_1$ maps $\I$ to itself up to reordering and rephasing, and right multiplication by $\Delta_2P_2$ only reorders and rephases the columns of $U$. For (ii), apply $U\dg$ to every basis: the pair becomes $(U\dg,\I)$. For (iii), take entrywise complex conjugates of every basis; this preserves $|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|$. Statement (iv) is (ii) followed by (iii). The final claim follows by applying $V\dg$ to every basis.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Fourier family}

Following~\cite[Eq.~(3)]{Jaming2009}, put $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$, $x=e^{2\pi ia}$, $y=e^{2\pi ib}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jaming}
\sqrt6\,F(a,b)=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&1&1&1&1&1\\
1&-\omega^2x&\omega&-x&\omega^2&-\omega x\\
1&\omega y&\omega^2&y&\omega&\omega^2y\\
1&-1&1&-1&1&-1\\
1&\omega^2x&\omega&x&\omega^2&\omega x\\
1&-\omega y&\omega^2&-y&\omega&-\omega^2y
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
With $\rho=e^{i\pi/3}$, this is $(\rho^{T_{jk}}E_{jk})_{j,k=0}^5$, where $T_{jk}=jk\bmod 6$ is the exponent table of the order-six Fourier matrix (so $\rho^{T_{jk}}$ is its $(j,k)$ entry) and $E_{jk}$ equals $x$ for $(j,k)\in\{1,4\}\times\{1,3,5\}$, $y$ for $(j,k)\in\{2,5\}\times\{1,3,5\}$, and $1$ otherwise.

\begin{theorem}[{\cite[Thm.~1.4]{Jaming2009}}]\label{thm:jaming}
For all $a,b\in\mathbb R$, the pair $(\I,F(a,b))$ is not extendable.
\end{theorem}

By \cref{lem:pair-ops}, the same conclusion holds for every unitary $U$ with $\sqrt6\,U^{T}\simeq\sqrt6\,F(a,b)$, i.e.\ for every member of the transposed Fourier family.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$H_2$}{H2}-reducibility and the status of a count criterion}

A $2\times2$ submatrix of a CHM is a \emph{Hadamard submatrix} if it is proportional to a unitary matrix. A CHM of order six is \emph{$H_2$-reducible} if, after equivalence, all nine blocks of its $3\times3$ block partition into $2\times2$ blocks are Hadamard submatrices. Karlsson showed that these are exactly the members of $K_6^{(3)}$~\cite{KarlssonH2,Karlsson2011}.

\begin{remark}[Status of the submatrix-count obstruction]\label{rem:chen-status}
Chen et al. state that an $H_2$-reducible matrix in a MUB quartet has exactly nine Hadamard $2\times2$ submatrices, which they use to exclude the \Dita{} family~\cite{Chen2021}. McNulty and Weigert show that the argument relies on an earlier lemma whose proof has a gap~\cite{McNultyWeigert2025Comment}. Liang and Chen rule out some other possible counts, including $10$--$16$ and $18$, but state that counts $17,19,20,\ldots$ still need to be excluded to establish the claimed theorem, and that they cannot presently save it~\cite{LiangChen2025Reply}. We treat this count criterion as unresolved and do not use it to infer non-extendability.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Karlsson's family and the \Dita{} slice}

Karlsson's family is~\cite{Karlsson2011}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:karlsson}
K_6(\theta,\phi,\lambda)=
\begin{bmatrix}
F_2 & Z_1 & Z_2\\
Z_3 & \tfrac12Z_3AZ_1 & \tfrac12Z_3BZ_2\\
Z_4 & \tfrac12Z_4BZ_1 & \tfrac12Z_4AZ_2
\end{bmatrix},\qquad
F_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad B=-F_2-A,
\end{equation}
where $Z_j=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\z_j&-z_j\end{pmatrix}$ for $j=1,2$, $Z_k=\begin{pmatrix}1&z_k\\1&-z_k\end{pmatrix}$ for $k=3,4$, $|z_i|=1$, $z_1=e^{i\lambda}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ablock}
A=\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}&A_{12}\\\overline{A_{12}}&-\overline{A_{11}}\end{pmatrix},\quad
\begin{aligned}
A_{11}&=-\tfrac12+\tfrac{i\sqrt3}{2}\bigl(\cos\theta+e^{-i\phi}\sin\theta\bigr),\\
A_{12}&=-\tfrac12+\tfrac{i\sqrt3}{2}\bigl(-\cos\theta+e^{i\phi}\sin\theta\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For generic parameters, $z_2,z_3,z_4$ are fixed by M\"obius relations $z_3^2=\mathcal M_A(z_1^2)$, $z_4^2=\mathcal M_B(z_1^2)$, $z_3^2=\mathcal M_B(z_2^2)$, $z_4^2=\mathcal M_A(z_2^2)$, where $\mathcal M_X(w)=(\alpha_Xw-\beta_X)/(\overline{\beta_X}w-\overline{\alpha_X})$ with $\alpha_X=X_{12}^2$ and $\beta_X=X_{11}^2$~\cite{Karlsson2011,McNultyWeigert2026}.

At $\theta_D=\arccos(1/\sqrt3)$ and $\phi=\pi/4$, \eqref{eq:Ablock} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:AB-dita}
A=\begin{pmatrix}i&-1\\-1&i\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}-1-i&0\\0&1-i\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[The \Dita{} slice in closed form]\label{prop:slice}
For $|z|=1$ let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dz}
D(z)=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&1&1&1&1&1\\
1&-1&z&-z&i&-i\\
1&-i&i&i&-i&-1\\
1&i&-z&z&-1&-i\\
1&\bar z&-i&-1&-\bar z&i\\
1&-\bar z&-1&-i&\bar z&i
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D(z)$ is the branch of \eqref{eq:karlsson} with $A,B$ as in \eqref{eq:AB-dita} and $(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)=(z,i,-i,\bar z)$. It is a CHM for every $|z|=1$, and it is $H_2$-reducible.
\item For every $|z|=1$, at least $51$ of the $225$ $2\times2$ submatrices of $D(z)$ are Hadamard submatrices; exactly $51$ for generic $z$, and $75$ at $z=1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) Block multiplication with \eqref{eq:AB-dita} gives $\tfrac12Z_3AZ_1=\begin{pmatrix}i&i\\-z&z\end{pmatrix}$, $\tfrac12Z_3BZ_2=\begin{pmatrix}-i&-1\\-1&-i\end{pmatrix}$, $\tfrac12Z_4BZ_1=\begin{pmatrix}-i&-1\\-1&-i\end{pmatrix}$ (using $z_4z_1=1$) and $\tfrac12Z_4AZ_2=\begin{pmatrix}-\bar z&i\\\bar z&i\end{pmatrix}$, which assemble to \eqref{eq:Dz}. The Gram matrix $D(z)D(z)\dg$ has entries that are Laurent polynomials in $z$ of degree at most one in $z$ and $\bar z=z^{-1}$; expanding them gives $6\I$ identically. Each of the nine blocks is visibly a Hadamard submatrix, so $D(z)$ is $H_2$-reducible.

(2) For rows $r<r'$ and columns $c<c'$, the submatrix is Hadamard exactly when $D_{rc}\overline{D_{r'c}}+D_{rc'}\overline{D_{r'c'}}=0$. This is a Laurent polynomial in $z$, and it vanishes identically for exactly $51$ of the $225$ choices. The remaining ones vanish only at finitely many $z$; at $z=1$ we count $75$.

\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:dita-class}
$D(1)$ has entries in $\{\pm1,\pm i\}$, and its Haagerup invariant multiset coincides numerically with that of \Dita{}'s matrix $D_6$~\cite{Dita2004}, consistent with the identification of this slice with $D_6^{(1)}$ in~\cite{McNultyWeigert2026}. This comparison is evidence for the identification at the displayed point; it does not establish equivalence of every $D(z)$ to a single representative, nor does the exact submatrix count give an extension obstruction in view of \cref{rem:chen-status}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Degeneracy of the M\"obius parametrisation]\label{rem:mobius}
On the \Dita{} slice $\alpha_A=1$ and $\beta_A=-1$, so $\alpha_Aw-\beta_A=w+1=-(\overline{\beta_A}w-\overline{\alpha_A})$, and hence $\mathcal M_A\equiv-1$ is constant (and undefined at $w=-1$). Thus $z_1$ does \emph{not} determine $z_4$ through $\mathcal M_A$, and the relation $z_4^2=\mathcal M_A(z_2^2)$ is the indeterminate form $0/0$ at $z_2^2=-1$. Similarly, at $\theta=0$ one finds $\mathcal M_A\equiv\mathcal M_B\equiv1$. Statements that $z_1$ determines the remaining parameters uniquely~\cite{McNultyWeigert2026} therefore need qualification on both slices. The closed form \eqref{eq:Dz} avoids the issue: it is verified directly, not derived from the M\"obius relations.
\end{remark}

\section{The exact triple}\label{sec:main}
%=====================================================================

Let $q=e^{i\pi/18}$, a primitive $36$th root of unity, so $q^{12}-q^6+1=0$. Then $\rho=q^6$, $\omega=q^{12}$ and $i=q^9$. Let $a,b,c\in\C$ satisfy $323a^2=d_a(q)$, $323b^2=d_b(q)$, $323c^2=d_c(q)$, where
\begin{align*}
d_a(q)&=-16q^{11}+35q^{10}-170q^9+18q^8-120q^7-32q^6\\&\qquad+166q^5+157q^4-8q^3+46q^2+48q+66,\\
d_b(q)&=166q^{11}-192q^{10}-170q^9+46q^8+72q^7-32q^6\\&\qquad-150q^5+35q^4-8q^3-64q^2-120q+66,\\
d_c(q)&=-150q^{11}+157q^{10}-170q^9-64q^8+48q^7-32q^6\\&\qquad-16q^5-192q^4-8q^3+18q^2+72q+66.
\end{align*}
The choice of square roots does not matter (see below). Define $M=[v_0\ \cdots\ v_5]$ with
\begin{align*}
v_0&=(1,\,a,\,-qa,\,q^{-10},\,q^{-16}a,\,q^{-8})^{T}, &
v_1&=(1,\,-a,\,qa,\,q^{-10},\,q^{2}a,\,q^{-8})^{T},\\
v_2&=(1,\,b,\,q^7b,\,q^2,\,q^{-4}b,\,q^{16})^{T}, &
v_3&=(1,\,-b,\,-q^7b,\,q^2,\,-q^{-4}b,\,q^{16})^{T},\\
v_4&=(1,\,c,\,q^{-5}c,\,q^{14},\,q^8c,\,q^4)^{T}, &
v_5&=(1,\,-c,\,-q^{-5}c,\,q^{14},\,-q^8c,\,q^4)^{T},
\end{align*}
and put $B=M/\sqrt6$ and $W=D(\rho)\dg M/\sqrt6$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
With the notation above:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\{\I,\ D(\rho)/\sqrt6,\ B\}$ is a MUB triple; equivalently (since $D(\rho)$ is a CHM) $M$ and $W$ are CHMs.
\item \textbf{(Fourier edge $\I\to B$.)} Let $p=(2,0,4,3,1,5)$ and $s=(4,3,1,0,2,5)$ (zero-based), let $N_{jk}=M_{s_k,p_j}$, and dephase: $C_{jk}=N_{jk}N_{00}/(N_{j0}N_{0k})$. Then $C=\sqrt6\,F(a_1,b_1)$ exactly, with
\[
x_1=e^{2\pi ia_1}=q^{30}\,\frac{b}{a},\qquad y_1=e^{2\pi ib_1}=q^{24}\,\frac{b}{c}.
\]
Hence $M^{T}\simeq\sqrt6\,F(a_1,b_1)$.
\item \textbf{(Fourier edge $D(\rho)\to B$.)} With $p'=(0,4,2,1,5,3)$, $s'=(0,1,2,5,4,3)$ and $N'_{jk}=W_{s'_k,p'_j}$, the dephased matrix $C'$ equals $\sqrt6\,F(a_2,b_2)$ exactly, for unimodular $x_2,y_2\in\Q(q,a,b,c)$ (given in \cref{app:exact}). Hence $W^{T}\simeq\sqrt6\,F(a_2,b_2)$.
\item \textbf{(Factorisation.)} $D(\rho)=M\,W\dg/\sqrt6$: the \Dita{}-slice matrix is, up to scale, a product of two Hadamard matrices whose transposes lie in the Fourier family.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Unimodularity.} Since $d_\bullet$ has rational coefficients and $|q|=1$, we have $\overline{d_\bullet(q)}=d_\bullet(q^{-1})$. Reduction modulo $q^{12}-q^6+1$ gives $d_\bullet(q)\,d_\bullet(q^{-1})=323^2$ for $\bullet\in\{a,b,c\}$, so $|a|=|b|=|c|=1$, and every entry of $M$ is unimodular. In particular $\bar a=a\,\overline{a^2}$, and similarly for $b$ and $c$, so complex conjugation acts on $\Q(q,a,b,c)$ by $q\mapsto q^{-1}$, $a\mapsto a\,d_a(q^{-1})/323$, and so on.

\emph{Item 1.} All required identities live in the field $\Q(q)(a,b,c)$, whose degree over $\Q$ is at most $12\cdot2^3=96$. We represent its elements as $\sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^3}\kappa_\epsilon(q)\,a^{\epsilon_1}b^{\epsilon_2}c^{\epsilon_3}$ with $\kappa_\epsilon\in\Q[q]/(q^{12}-q^6+1)$ and reduce $a^2,b^2,c^2$ by the defining relations. In this representation, exact rational arithmetic gives: $v_j\dg v_k=0$ for all $j<k$ (15 identities); $|D(\rho)_{:,j}\dg v_k|^2=6$ for all $j,k$ (36 identities); and $D(\rho)D(\rho)\dg=6\I$. Hence $M/\sqrt6$ is unitary and unbiased to both $\I$ and $D(\rho)/\sqrt6$, and $W$ is a CHM.

\emph{Items 2 and 3.} All entries of $N$ and $N'$ are nonzero (both matrices have unimodular entries), so the dephased matrices are defined. Requiring $C=\sqrt6F(a_1,b_1)$ amounts to: $C_{jk}=\rho^{T_{jk}}$ at the 24 parameter-free positions, and $C_{jk}/\rho^{T_{jk}}$ constant on each of the two parameter sets of \eqref{eq:jaming}. After clearing denominators these become polynomial identities in $\Q(q)(a,b,c)$, and we verified all of them exactly for both $N$ and $N'$. The displayed values of $x_1,y_1$ follow by evaluating $C_{11}/\rho$ and $C_{21}/\rho^2$. All parameters are unimodular, because they are ratios of unimodular numbers. Dephasing and the two permutations are monomial operations, and $N$ is the permuted transpose of $M$, so $M^{T}\simeq C$; likewise $W^{T}\simeq C'$.

\emph{Item 4.} By definition $W=D(\rho)\dg M/\sqrt6$. Since $D(\rho)D(\rho)\dg=6\I$, this gives $D(\rho)W=\sqrt6\,M$, and since $WW\dg=6\I$, it gives $D(\rho)=M W\dg/\sqrt6$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Item~3 is what makes the triple a ``triangle'' with two Fourier edges. Changing the sign of $a$, $b$ or $c$ swaps the columns of one pair $(v_{2k},v_{2k+1})$, so the basis is independent of the choice of square roots.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[The Fourier edges are obstructed]\label{cor:three-edges}
The pairs $(\I,B)$ and $(D(\rho)/\sqrt6,B)$ are non-extendable. Consequently, the triple of \cref{thm:main} is not contained in any MUB quartet. This argument does not decide whether the third pair $(\I,D(\rho)/\sqrt6)$ is extendable.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $(\I,B)$, apply \cref{thm:main}(2), the equivalence operations in \cref{lem:pair-ops}, and \cref{thm:jaming}. For $(D(\rho)/\sqrt6,B)$, \cref{lem:pair-ops} reduces extendability to that of $(\I,W/\sqrt6)$; then use \cref{thm:main}(3) and \cref{thm:jaming}. A quartet containing the triple would extend each pair, so either obstruction rules out such a quartet.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to known triples]
Jaming et al.\ construct triples $(\I,F(0,b(t)),C(t))$~\cite[Thm.~2.4]{Jaming2009}. To compare, we used the power sums $\sum\lambda^m$ ($m\le12$) of the Haagerup invariants $\lambda=h_{jk}h_{lm}\overline{h_{jm}h_{lk}}$. These are invariant under equivalence and transposition, and the line $a=0$ is closed under complex conjugation. Numerically, neither $F(a_1,b_1)$ nor $F(a_2,b_2)$ matches any member of the line $a=0$: for $F(a_1,b_1)$, the differences in the sixth and twelfth power sums vanish at different values of $b$. The triple is therefore \emph{not} one of the triples of~\cite[Thm.~2.4]{Jaming2009}. This comparison is numerical evidence rather than a proof.
\end{remark}

\section{Numerical evidence on the \Dita{} slice}\label{sec:numerics}
%=====================================================================

The computations in this section are finite and numerical. They describe what was recovered, not what exists.

\paragraph{Method.} For a fixed CHM $H$, vectors unbiased to both $\I$ and $H$ are written in the gauge $v_0=1$ as $z_k=\sqrt6\,v_k$. Replacing $\bar z_k$ by independent variables $w_k$ gives five equations $z_kw_k=1$ and five independent unbiasedness equations (the sixth follows from Parseval). We solve this system by homotopy continuation~\cite{HomotopyContinuation}, keep the roots on the real locus $w_k=\bar z_k$, check them against the original equations, and remove duplicates. Orthogonal pairs among the retained vectors define a graph, and its $6$-cliques are candidate third bases. For each candidate triple we tracked a square subsystem of the single-vector witness system for a fourth basis (mixed volume $252$) and filtered the endpoints against the full system.

At $\lambda=0$ the recovered pool size and ten third bases agree with the Dita-matrix search of Brierley and Weigert~\cite{Brierley2009}; their paper discusses the complete vector set identified earlier by Bengtsson et al.~\cite{BengtssonMUH2007}. In a dense scan of $628$ values $\lambda\in\{0,0.01,\dots,6.27\}$, every sample had a recovered $6$-clique. Nine high-precision perturbations off the slice with $|\Delta\phi|\ge10^{-3}$ lost the $6$-clique. The striking feature is the last column of \cref{tab:numerics}: every recovered third basis forms a Fourier--Fourier--\Dita{} triangle, as the exact triple of \cref{thm:main} does.

The exact construction at $\lambda=\pi/3$ in \cref{thm:main} is an algebraic third basis with exact Fourier charts, but we have not proved that it coincides with any of the four bases recovered numerically at that parameter. For the other six sampled parameter values, no exact closed forms for the recovered third bases were obtained. Thus all 40 identifications in the table remain numerical fits, and none of the seven recovered pools is proved complete.

The exactification attempts are limited. One recovered clique at $\lambda=\pi/3$ has a 110-digit numerical coordinate export, and the exact ansatz is numerically consistent with it at about $10^{-100}$. Bounded PSLQ returned candidate relations, but no minimal polynomials or common number field were certified, so exact identity with that stored clique has not been proved. At $\lambda=0.4$, an LLL search in a small multiquadratic field used cached Float64 coordinates and matched only the six trivial first-row entries, so it gives no exact reconstruction. The other 38 recovered bases have no high-precision coordinate exports in the current data. A systematic PSLQ/LLL reconstruction of those bases has therefore not been carried out.

\begin{conjecture}[Triangle conjecture]\label{conj:triangle}
For every $|z|=1$ and every orthonormal basis $X$ unbiased to both $\I$ and $D(z)/\sqrt6$, both transitions $\sqrt6X$ and $D(z)\dg X$ are, up to transposition, equivalent to members of the Fourier family $F(a,b)$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}
If proved, \cref{conj:triangle} together with \cref{thm:jaming} would imply that every such triple is non-extendable: each of its two edges incident to a third basis would be a Fourier-family pair. The conjecture would not decide extendability of the pair $(\I,D(z)/\sqrt6)$ by itself. It would provide a structural description of MUB triples through $D_6^{(1)}$ and exact input for the Hadamard-cube programme of~\cite{Matolcsi2025}. A proof requires (a) completeness of the third-basis pool and (b) exact identification of its members. The recognition criterion of~\cite{MatszangoszSzollosi2024} has additional hypotheses: it concerns a normalized CHM with three distinct columns each containing an entry $-1$, and its conclusion allows a 2-circulant outcome as well as the transposed Fourier family. We have not certified these hypotheses for the numerically recovered candidates. The fits reported here do not establish exact membership.
\end{remark}

%=====================================================================
\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
%=====================================================================

The exact triple of \cref{thm:main} has two proved non-extendable edges, both identified with the Fourier family; either one rules out a quartet containing the triple. The status of the third pair $(\I,D(\rho)/\sqrt6)$ remains open here. The factorisation in \cref{thm:main} explains how this \Dita{}-slice matrix occurs between two transposed Fourier-family transitions. The numerical evidence suggests this pattern may recur along the slice, but it does not establish that claim.

An open problem is the extension question for pairs in the generic part of Karlsson's family. At the single tested point $K_6(0.3,0.5,0.2)$, we count nine Hadamard $2\times2$ submatrices and recovered no third basis (the largest recovered clique had size two). This count neither invokes nor satisfies a proven extension obstruction: the cited count criterion is unresolved, and the finite numerical pool is not certified complete. Establishing completeness at a specific algebraic parameter, for example by certified homotopy continuation or exact elimination, would be needed before a negative search result could imply non-extendability.

\section{A misprint in a printed form of Karlsson's \texorpdfstring{$A$}{A}-block}\label{app:erratum}
%=====================================================================

In~\cite[Eqs.~(7.3)--(7.5)]{McNultyWeigert2026} (arXiv:2410.23997v2), the block is printed as
\[
A_{\mathrm{MW}}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{\mathrm{MW}}_{11}&A_{12}\\A_{12}&-A^{\mathrm{MW}}_{11}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A^{\mathrm{MW}}_{11}=+\tfrac12+\tfrac{i\sqrt3}{2}\bigl(\cos\theta+e^{-i\phi}\sin\theta\bigr),
\]
with $A_{12}$ as in \eqref{eq:Ablock}. This differs from Karlsson's \eqref{eq:Ablock} in the sign of the constant term of $A_{11}$, and in the missing complex conjugations in the second row.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:misprint}
With $\Delta=\sqrt3\sin\theta\sin\phi$ and $\Gamma=\sin\theta\cos\phi\,(\sqrt3+3\sin\theta\sin\phi)$,
\[
A_{\mathrm{MW}}A_{\mathrm{MW}}\dg-2\I=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta&-i\Gamma\\ i\Gamma&\Delta\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For real $\theta,\phi$ this vanishes if and only if $\sin\theta=0$, whereas Karlsson's block satisfies $AA\dg=2\I$ identically. Hence the printed block fails the identity $AA\dg=2\I$ required in Karlsson's construction whenever $\sin\theta\ne0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct expansion. If $\sin\theta\ne0$, then $\Delta=0$ forces $\sin\phi=0$, and then $\Gamma=\sqrt3\sin\theta\cos\phi\ne0$.
\end{proof}

\section{An independent Gr\"obner check at \texorpdfstring{$D_6$}{D6}}\label{app:groebner}
%=====================================================================

\begin{proposition}[reported computation]\label{prop:groebner}
Let $D_{\mathrm{bc}}$ be a block-circulant representative of $D_6$ and $F_D$ the third basis of~\cite{BengtssonMUH2007}. The single-vector witness ideal for a fourth basis (five relations $z_kw_k=1$ and twelve unbiasedness equations in $\Q(\zeta_{24},\sqrt5)[z_1,\dots,z_5,w_1,\dots,w_5]$) is the unit ideal. Hence no vector is unbiased to $\I$, $D_{\mathrm{bc}}$ and $F_D$ simultaneously.
\end{proposition}

The field has degree $16$: $[\Q(\zeta_{24}):\Q]=\varphi(24)=8$. The prime $73$ splits completely in $\Q(\zeta_{24})$ because $73\equiv1\pmod{24}$, whereas $5$ is not a square modulo $73$; hence $\sqrt5\notin\Q(\zeta_{24})$, and adjoining it doubles the degree. The computation was performed in Macaulay2 (version 1.24.05), and the reduced Gr\"obner basis is reported to be $\{1\}$. This re-verifies one fixed instance already covered by Brierley and Weigert's stronger pool result~\cite{Brierley2009}; it is included as an independent cross-check and has not been rerun for this version.
